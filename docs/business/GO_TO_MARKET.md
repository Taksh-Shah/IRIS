## 1. GTM Philosophy

IRIS follows a **trust-first, deployment-second** go-to-market. In the disaster communication market, trust is the primary purchase criterion. Government and enterprise buyers will not deploy communication infrastructure that is: (a) proprietary and unauditable, (b) untested in Indian conditions, or (c) dependent on foreign vendors.

IRIS builds trust through:
- Open source (Apache 2.0) — protocol is auditable
- India-first spectrum compliance — deployed legally
- Field trials with NDRF — tested in real conditions
- Academic research partnerships — protocol is validated

Revenue follows trust. The GTM does not optimize for early revenue at the cost of trust-building.

## 2. Phase 1: Foundation (Year 1, Months 1–12)

### 2.1 Government Pilot Partnerships

**Goal:** Establish NDRF as the anchor government partner; produce a field trial result that can be cited in all future government sales.

**Actions:**
- Month 1–3: Introductory meetings with NDRF Inspector General and NDMA officials. Present concept paper: "IRIS: India's Open Emergency Communication Mesh"
- Month 3–6: Technical demonstration at NDRF training center (Pune/Nagpur facility). Deploy 10-device test mesh for live demonstration.
- Month 6–9: Pilot MoU signed with NDRF 12th Battalion (Gandhinagar, Gujarat). Scope: 50-device pilot over 3 months.
- Month 9–12: Pilot execution; data collection; NDRF evaluation report.

**Target outcome:** NDMA endorsement letter stating IRIS is suitable for emergency communication deployments.

### 2.2 Open Source Community Launch

**Goal:** Establish IRIS as a serious, well-documented open source project with an active community.

**Actions:**
- Month 1: GitHub repository public (IRIS core, Android app, edge server — all Apache 2.0)
- Month 2: Technical blog post: "IRIS Protocol: How We Built a Disruption-Tolerant Mesh for Indian Disasters" (published on Engineering blog + dev.to + HackerNews)
- Month 3: Talk at rootconf (India's premier DevOps/infra conference) — audience: engineers who become community members and potential hires
- Month 6: Talk at FOSSASIA — international open source community visibility
- Ongoing: Monthly engineering blog posts on protocol design, routing algorithms, India-specific challenges

**Target outcome:** 500+ GitHub stars; 50+ community members; 5+ external contributors.

### 2.3 Research Community Engagement

**Actions:**
- Submit to IIT Bombay / IISc for academic collaboration (see RESEARCH_METHODOLOGY.md)
- Present research questions at ACM SIGCOMM India workshop (if available)
- Submit first academic paper: "PRoPHET Parameter Calibration for Indian Disaster Scenarios"

**Target outcome:** 1 accepted academic paper; 2 institutional research partnerships.

## 3. Phase 2: Growth (Year 2, Months 13–24)

### 2.1 App Store Launch

**Goal:** Public availability on Play Store and App Store for civilian use; builds brand awareness and creates real-world test deployments.

**Actions:**
- Month 13: Google Play Store public launch (free, open source)
- Month 15: Apple App Store public launch
- Month 16: F-Droid listing (open source Android app store)
- Press: tech press coverage (Economic Times Tech, The Hindu Tech, TechCrunch India)
- Partnership with disaster preparedness NGOs for user acquisition

**Target outcome:** 10,000 downloads in first 3 months; 500 active relay nodes in India.

### 3.2 NGO Partnerships

**Goal:** NGOs provide high-visibility disaster deployments that validate IRIS in real scenarios.

**Target NGOs:**
- Oxfam India (flood response programs in Assam, Bihar)
- World Vision India (disaster response network — 15 state offices)
- India Red Cross (volunteers in 35 states)
- Aga Khan Rural Support Programme (Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh rural communities)

**Partnership model:** IRIS provides free hardware kits (NGO pricing) + support; NGOs provide deployment data and testimonials. No revenue in Year 1 from NGO partners; relationship investment.

### 3.3 Disaster Preparedness Training Market

**Goal:** Integrate IRIS training into existing disaster preparedness training curricula.

**Targets:**
- NDMA Aapda Mitra program (1M+ trained volunteers) — propose IRIS module
- SDMA training programs in Gujarat, Odisha, Tamil Nadu
- Corporate EHS training providers (BSI India, DNV India)

**Revenue model:** Training material licensing + IRIS-certified trainer program (₹25,000 per trainer certification).

## 4. Phase 3: Scale (Year 3, Months 25–36)

### 4.1 Enterprise Sales

**Goal:** Convert enterprise POCs from Year 2 into full contracts; build pipeline in mining, construction, oil & gas.

**Actions:**
- Hire 2 enterprise sales executives (mining/O&G background)
- Attend EHS trade shows: SAFETY India, Mining World India, ADIPEC (oil & gas)
- Partner with safety management system providers (SAP EHS integration)
- Case study publication: "How [Mining Company] reduced emergency response time by 40% with IRIS"

**Target outcome:** 5 enterprise accounts (200+ devices each); ₹1.5 crore ARR from enterprise.

### 4.2 International Expansion: South Asia

**Goal:** Expand to Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal — highest geographic similarity to India; shared disaster risk (Bay of Bengal cyclones, Himalayan seismicity).

**Priority markets:**
- **Bangladesh:** High cyclone risk (Cyclone Sidr 2007, Amphan 2020); government (BDRCS, DDMC) as first target; similarity to India market
- **Nepal:** High earthquake risk (2015 Gorkha earthquake); UNDP and INGOs as entry point
- **Sri Lanka:** Tsunami risk; disaster management authority engagement

**Entry approach:** Partner with local disaster management organizations; localize to Bengali/Sinhala/Nepali; no direct sales team in Year 3 (partner-led).

### 4.3 Channel Partner Program Launch

**Goal:** Enable system integrators and EHS consultants to sell and deploy IRIS without IRIS involvement in every deal.

**Program structure:**
- **Certified Reseller:** Authorized to resell hardware kits; 15% margin
- **Certified Integrator:** Authorized to deploy and configure; 20% margin + ₹2 lakh training fee
- **Certified Partner:** Can customize and extend IRIS; co-sell with IRIS team; 25% margin

**Target:** 10 certified resellers, 5 certified integrators by Month 36.

## 5. Marketing Channels

### 5.1 Content Marketing

**Engineering blog (iris-comms.in/blog):**
- Audience: engineers, researchers, technically sophisticated buyers
- Cadence: 2 posts/month
- Topics: protocol design, India-specific challenges, field trial results, academic research

**Policy blog:**
- Audience: government officials, NGO leadership, policy researchers
- Cadence: 1 post/month
- Topics: regulatory landscape, DPDPA, spectrum, disaster communication policy

**Social media:**
- LinkedIn: primary channel for B2B and B2G (government officials are active on LinkedIn)
