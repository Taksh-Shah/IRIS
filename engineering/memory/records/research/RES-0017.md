## 1. Question 1 — Emergency alert broadcast formats & standards

### Findings

- **OASIS CAP v1.2 (2010-07-01, OASIS Standard)** is the alert-message *lingua franca*: `event/urgency/severity/certainty/expires` semantics + `info`/`area` blocks. Adopted by FEMA IPAWS, EU-Alert, and ITU-T. CAP itself is XML — **not usable on a 255-byte LoRa envelope**; it is the *semantic reference*, not the wire format. Level 1. https://docs.oasis-open.org/emergency/cap/v1.2/CAP-v1.2-os.html
- **ITU-T X.1303 / X.1303bis** encode CAP-alert semantics in ASN.1; X.1303bis adds the **compact binary encoding (XER↔PER)** — evidence the industry wants a compact CAP variant, but it is ASN.1/PER, not CBOR, and not interoperable with the IRIS flat CBOR envelope. Level 1. https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-X.1303bis
- **EU-Alert — ETSI TS 102 900 V1.4.1 (2023-06-19)**: CAP-based European Public Warning System delivered over **Cell Broadcast** + SMS (incl. location-based SMS). **§5.5 explicitly states Cell Broadcast provides NO subscriber/UE authentication** — the single most important limitation for authority-broadcast design (see Q2/Q7). Level 1. https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_ts/102900_102999/102900/01.04.01_60/ts_102900v010401p.pdf
- **Cell Broadcast itself = 3GPP TS 23.041** (Technical Realization of the Cell Broadcast Service) — broadcast carried by the cellular network, unauthenticated to the UE at application layer. Level 1. https://portal.3gpp.org/desktopmodules/Specifications/SpecificationDetails.aspx?specificationId=760
- **WEA 3.0 — ATIS-0700041.v002**: US Wireless Emergency Alerts device-behavior spec; adds **Device-Based Geo-Fencing** (on-device location filtering of alerts) — directly validates IRIS's `area_code`-scoped gating and `DisasterModeActivation.area_code` design. Level 1 (ATIS standard; pin exact document number at DESIGN). https://www.atis.org/
- **FEMA IPAWS**: operational CAP profile practice (public safety authorities author alerts via CAP). Level 3 (practice). https://www.fema.gov/emergency-managers/practitioners/integrated-public-alert-warning-system
- **India — NRTA emergency-broadcast standard package**: the State Administration of Radio and Television published a 12-standard package (广电发〔2023〕54号) incl. **GY/T 383-2023 应急广播系统总体技术规范** (overall technical specification) and **GY/T 385-2023 应急广播消息格式规范** (message format); earlier series **GD/J 082-2018** (message format); later **GY/T 426-2026** (active-publish terminal). These define wired/broadcast/CATV/IP emergency radio + terminals — **not** a device-to-device mesh packet standard (no such national D2D mesh alert standard was found in the source set). Level 1 (national standards; standard numbers verified via standards-registry search 2026-08-15; deep-link to NRTA portal unavailable in session — retrieve at DESIGN).
- **No standardized "CAP in CBOR" exists**: search for "SEP-0182"/CAP-CBOR/CSD-0182 returned no standard, RFC, IETF draft, or industry spec (see Gap G1). IRIS therefore designs its own minimal CBOR subset using CAP field semantics.

### Implication for design

Emergency broadcast format = **minimal CAP-inspired CBOR subset**, CBOR-encoded per the existing IRIS flat envelope (RFC 8949 §4.2 canonical), not CAP-XML, not ASN.1/PER. Geographic gating via `area_code` is standard practice (WEA 3.0 device geo-fencing). No mesh alert standard exists to conform to — IRIS conformance target is the *semantic model* (CAP) with an IRIS-native encoding.

## 2. Question 2 — Emergency broadcast authentication & integrity

### Findings

- **Cell-Broadcast alert systems provide NO UE authentication** (ETSI TS 102 900 §5.5) — hence the WEA spoofing body of research:
  - **CACM 2023 "Securing the Wireless Emergency Alerts System"** (Ha et al.): demonstrates practical WEA alert spoofing and proposes mitigation: **Ed25519 signatures + SIM-provisioned key**, ≈68-byte overhead per alert. This is the closest deployed-world precedent to the IRIS authority model (offline-verifiable Ed25519 + pre-provisioned trust anchor). Level 2. https://cacm.acm.org/news/securing-the-wireless-emergency-alerts-system/
  - **ACM 2024 "Fixing Insecure Cellular System Information Broadcasts For Good"** (Broadcast But Verify): LTE system-information broadcast verification with **3.220 ms measured overhead** — verification of broadcast content on constrained/cheap radios is practical. Level 2.
  - **arXiv 2604.24404 (2026-04) "From Spoofing to Trust"**: 5G SA spoofing testbed + cross-cell verification results — confirms the attack is still live on 5G and only being mitigated 15 years after first reports. Level 2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24404
- **SPKI is the correct offline chain model**: RFC 9804 (2025-06, Informational) re-ratifies SPKI/SDSI: the certificate **subject is a key** ("keyholder"), and chains of authorization are key-signed. This matches (a) IDENT-001 RED-0008 `verify_chain` (key-anchored, cap 8, no PKIX) and (b) the EMERGENCY_GOVERNANCE hierarchy (National→State→District→Local, max depth 4). Level 1. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9804.html
- **Meshtastic — lessons on mesh auth fragility**:
  - Channel encryption does **not** authenticate identity: any PSK holder can spoof any node (Meshtastic SECURITY.md). Level 4. https://meshtastic.org/docs/software/security/
  - **CVE-2025-52464** (2025, **CVSSv4 9.5**): cloned keypairs from low-entropy / key-material exposure; patched in 2.6.11 — direct evidence that weak key hygiene breaks mesh identity. Level 1 (advisory DB). https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-52464
  - New signing (XEdDSA) adds **64-byte signatures vs a 256-byte packet limit** — the same signature-vs-size tradeoff IRIS faces (DISC-0008: 64-byte sig dominates the 237-byte P0 envelope; payload ceiling 84 B). Level 4.
- **Firm, preferable validation of IRIS's design**: preloaded root + offline key-anchored chain + Ed25519 verify at every relay is exactly the mitigation the WEA literature proposes for cellular, applied to a mesh where there is *no* SIM/identity provider. The EMERGENCY_BROADCAST.md `verify_and_relay_broadcast` pattern (dedup → expiry → chain verify → sig verify → forward-all; no error to sender = anti-probing) is sound. **Mandatory relay of *verified* broadcasts** is the correct anti-starvation rule (a node may only drop unverifiable broadcasts).

### Implication for design

Reuse IDENT-001 SPKI/Ed25519 chain verification (RED-0008) for authority certificates. Verify at every relay; drop + log on any cert/sig failure (no reply = anti-probing); mandatory relay only for verified broadcasts. Do NOT rely on channel encryption for authenticity (Meshtastic/CVE-2025-52464 lesson).

## 3. Question 3 — Priority/preemption & low-power emergency signaling prior art

### Findings

- **802.11e / WMM EDCA**: 8 QoS traffic classes mapped to **4 access categories** (voice/video/best-effort/background) with AIFS/CW/TXOP differentiation → bounded preemption without starvation is established practice. IRIS's 7-priority model is finer-grained but the same family; P0-P3 precedence + query scheduling (RES-0010 Audsley/DRR) already provides the non-starvation property. Level 1. https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.11e/2275/
- **COSPAS-SARSAT 406 MHz beacons**: distress burst every **45–50 s**, **first burst deliberately delayed ≈50 s** (to favor higher-priority traffic on the same channel) — evidence that even dedicated distress signaling uses bounded cadence, not continuous TX. Contrast with IRIS P0 SOS: no regulatory mandate for 45-50 s cadence on LoRa; IRIS's own WPC 1% duty-cycle constraint is the binding external limit (FAIL-0001). Level 1 (COSPAS-SARSAT operational docs). https://www.cospas-sarsat.int/en/
- **Garmin inReach** (Iridium satellite messenger): tracking interval starts at 1-minute then relaxes to 10-minute — **cadence-based battery policy precedent** for emergency devices. Level 3 (vendor docs).
- **Disaster routing survey** (Springer SN Comp Sci, s42979-023-02509-2): surveys disaster-communication routing/wireless priority approaches for ad-hoc emergency nets — corroborates flood-with-hop-limit for safety-critical data. Level 2/3. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42979-023-02509-2
- **Energy-aware WMN** (IJCSRR 08(03):1312-1319, 2025-03): clustering/sleep-wake/transmit-power schemes for disaster mesh longevity — literature context for the DISASTER_MODES.md battery policy. Low-tier journal: treat as corroboration only. Level 5. https://ijcsrr.org/optimization-of-wireless-mesh-networks-for-disaster-response-communication
- **No external standard defines a "15-minute SOS retransmit cadence"**: the implemented behavior (ack.rs: P0 retry 30 s → 2 × → unlimited-until-TTL; P0 TTL 72 h) and DISASTER_MODES gossip convergence (30-90 s) is IRIS-specific; any doc claim of a fixed 15-min retransmit must be corrected to the implemented retry schedule (Conflict C5). Level 4 (own code).

### Implication for design

Priority queues are standard practice; keep the 7-tier model. SOS cadence is a *design* decision bounded by (a) WPC 1% duty cycle (never bypassed), (b) TTL 72 h, (c) battery — reference COSPAS-SARSAT (bounded cadence) and Garmin (cadence relaxation) as prior-art rationale; do not cite a nonexistent 15-min standard.

## 4. Question 4 — Disaster-mode network behavior (gossip, hops, storage eviction, battery, drills/testing)

### Findings (literature + practice now found; earlier gap closed)

- **Flood-to-hop-limit is the mesh baseline**: Meshtastic propagates by flooding, each node rebroadcasts up to a configurable hop limit (mesh broadcast algorithm). Level 4. https://meshtastic.org/docs/overview/mesh-algo
- **Hop-limit forwarding is actively refined**: Meshtastic firmware discussion #4764 ("Limit messages forwarded by routers depending on max hops settings") shows deployed practice: a solar router hitting European TX/duty limits chose **CLIENT role (wait + listen-before-rebroadcast) instead of always-Rebroadcast ROUTER**, cutting forwarded messages ~⅓. This validates (a) hop-trimmed forwarding for duty-cycle compliance, (b) the IRIS `ForwardedCache` dedup + gossip hop limits (5 auto / 255 authority) and (c) duty-cycle-aware forwarding — directly relevant to DISASTER_MODES "all transports at max" (must still respect WPC; FAIL-0001). Level 4/5. https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/discussions/4764
- **MeshCore** (regionmesh): "duplicate detection combined with hop counting eliminates loops" — same dedup+hop model as IRIS. Level 4. https://www.regionmesh.com/meshcore-routing-algorithms
- **Epistle** (IJRTI 2605165, 2026-05): offline-first P2P disaster messenger — hop count + max hop limit + seen-cache dedup + **persistent store-and-forward queue** + per-peer **rate limiter** + measured battery (~14%/2 h continuous). Design-pattern corroboration for IRIS (store-and-forward is STORE/SCF; rate limiting is DEC-EMERG-0005; battery ~30% scan cost is echoed in DISASTER_MODES). Low-tier journal: corroboration only. Level 5. https://ijrti.org/papers/IJRTI2605165.pdf
- **FirstNet / Built with AT&T** (US public-safety network): ~6M connections, 160+ sites, disaster-hardened build (hardened sites, backup power, deployable assets). Vendor-reported figures (Level 3) — validates the *concept* of a dedicated priority emergency network, not the mesh mechanism. https://www.firstnet.com/ ; https://www.att.com/
- **goTenna** (2021): tactical mobile-mesh for first responders after Hurricanes Sandy/Dorian/Maria/Forence — real-world deployment evidence for phone-scale mesh in disasters. Level 5 (vendor blog). https://gotenna.com/blogs/newsroom/mobile-mesh-networks-can-ensure-communication-in-disaster
- **Drills / exercise framework (for TEST_MODE design)**:
  - **FEMA HSEEP** (Homeland Security Exercise and Evaluation Program) + National Exercise Program + PrepToolkit: the official exercise methodology (seminar → workshop → tabletop → functional → full-scale). Level 1. https://www.fema.gov/emergency-managers/national-preparedness/exercises ; https://preptoolkit.fema.gov/
  - **FEMA Communications Drills guidance** (REPP Academy): monthly/quarterly/annual cadence for comms drills; test hardening, backup channels, and alternate tools. Level 1/2. https://preptoolkit.fema.gov/web/fema-repp-academy/communications-drills
  - **ISCRAM 2024 Roxborough Park Wildfire Evacuation Drill** (Proceedings, 2025-05-02): community-level evacuation drill with researcher observation — methodology for community-scale emergency-communication exercises. Level 3. https://ojs.iscram.org/index.php/Proceedings/article/view/122

### Implication for design

- Gossip hop limits (5 auto / 255 authority) are consistent with deployed mesh practice; keep.
- Storage eviction (disaster mode: P0-P2 dedicated pool ≤80%, P3 ≤20%, P4-P7 suspended/evicted) has no contradicting prior art — aligns with scf.rs LRU + P0-exempt policy; document as IRIS-specific.
- Battery policy (cutoff 15% → 3% in disaster; scan 5 s → 0.5 s) matches Epistle-style mesh power data (~30% scan cost is plausible; Level 5 estimate — benchmark at TEST).
- **TEST_MODE/drill capability**: EMERG-001 must support HSEEP-style exercises — marked/test-authority broadcasts, display suppression, and no real-alert side effects (a drill "evacuation" must never trigger WEA-like OS channels). This is a DESIGN requirement (DEC-EMERG-0008), not a research speculation.

## 5. Question 5 — BPv7/DTN status-report pattern (SOS ack + broadcast status)

### Findings

- **RFC 9171 §6.1** defines Bundle Status Reports: a status is an *administrative record* the bundle's custodian/endpoint inserts (reception, forwarding, delivery, deletion + status *flag* per bundle with time). BPv7 nodes use these to track a bundle through custody. Level 1. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9171.html
- **§6.2: status-report generation MUST be OFF by default** — enabling it consumes one bundle (the report) per bundle (the tracked one); the DTN community treats report overhead as a real cost. IRIS should do the same: **no per-message status reports by default**. Level 1.
- **RFC 9713 (2025-01)** establishes the IANA registry for Bundle Protocol **Admin Record Type codes** — the registry-based approach to administrative record types. Level 1. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9713.html
- **RFC 9758** (secure bundle C/C++ profile; normative-crypto alignment) — corroborates the secure-bundle trajectory. Level 1. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9758.html
- **arXiv 2507.17403 (2025-07, ESA)**: DTN custody-transfer with **compressed status signals in CBOR** — (a) confirms CBOR for compact reports, (b) confirms per-bundle report traffic is a first-class cost to minimize. Level 2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17403
- **Mapping to IRIS**:
  - SOS **delivery acknowledgment** (direct message path) ≡ BPv7 "delivery status" semantics, but IRIS-style: an explicit `Ack`/`SosAck` content message, not an envelope status bit.
  - Emergency **broadcast propagation is NOT status-reported** (flood: no per-relay ACK — anti-probing + bandwidth). Correct.
  - **Broadcast cancel/update** ≡ an *administrative record* in BPv7 terms (RFC 9713-style typed admin record): a signed, typed, authority-originated message (see DEC-EMERG-0004). This is the clean BPv7-aligned way to do `BroadcastCancel` without inventing a new envelope field.

### Implication for design

Keep status reporting off by default (RFC 9171 §6.2). SOS ack = application message; broadcast cancel = RFC 9713-style typed admin record. Any future per-bundle status feature must use compressed CBOR (arXiv 2507.17403) — not a v1 feature.

## 7. Question 7 — Legal/abuse & Sybil resistance

### Findings

- **BNS 2023 replaced IPC (effective 2024-07-01)**: the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (Act 45 of 2023) replaced the Indian Penal Code. Verified mappings from search: **IPC §420 → BNS §318** (cheating); **IPC §505 → BNS §353(2)** (circulating false/rumor). Level 1 (Act text via indiacode/AI-generated? — see honesty note: mapping sourced from secondary legal summaries; validate against the Act text at LEGAL-001). https://www.indiacode.nic.in/ ; https://www.mha.gov.in/
- **`EMERGENCY_ABUSE.md` legal references are STALE**: it cites IPC sections (420/505/153-A-class) — the abuse section must migrate IPC→BNS before VERIFY (Conflict C4; IPC §153A → BNS mapping unverified — Gap G3).
- **WEA spoofing is a real, long-lived abuse vector**: CACM 2023 + ACM 2024 + arXiv 2604.24404 (§2) show fake emergency alerts are controllable only with broadcast authentication — replicas of EMERGENCY_ABUSE scenario "fake authority broadcast". Level 1/2.
- **Sybil attacks make per-identity rate limits insufficient**:
  - **SybilGuard** (Yu et al., ACM CCS 2008) + **SybilLimit** (NUS): social-graph-based Sybil defense — only floor identity fabrication cost by requiring edges/trust. Level 2. https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/aspnes/pinewiki/attachments/SybilAttack/sybilguard.pdf
  - **Newsome et al. 2004** (sensor networks): nodes illegitimately claim identities; defenses = identity verification + resource testing. Level 2. https://netsec.ethz.ch/publications/papers/newsome_shi_song_perrig_sybil.pdf
  - **Wikipedia "Sybil attack"**: identity proxies (phone/SMS/IP) are cheaply massable; social-trust graphs (SybilGuard/SybilLimit/Advogato, SumUp/DSybil, Whānau) are the standard mitigations. Level 5 (supplementary overview). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sybil_attack
  - **Briar** (1.5.17, 2026-03-12): key-based identity, no phone number — the P2P baseline; no special Sybil countermeasure beyond verified-vs-unverified contact distinction (BQP QR pairing). Level 1 (project) / Level 2 (academic analyses). https://briarproject.org/
- **Consequence for IRIS**: a Sybil attacker can mint unlimited NodeIds cheaply (Ed25519 keygen is free) → (a) per-NodeId rate limits can be evaded by key rotation; (b) the *only* hard-resource gate is the **offline authority chain** for broadcasts and **geographic clustering** for SOS (DISASTER_MODES Signal 1), plus dedup/seen-cache. Absolute Sybil resistance is impossible on an open permissionless mesh; the goal is *bounded damage* (no spoofed broadcasts; SOS flood only affects its own area; storage eviction protects P0-P2). Level 1/2.

### Implication for design

Rate limits at message-engine layer per NodeId (with Sybil caveat); **never** rate-limit verified authority broadcasts; rely on dedup + area-clustering + authority-chain verification — not per-identity caps — for broadcast integrity. Migrate EMERGENCY_ABUSE.md to BNS before VERIFY (DEC-EMERG-0007).

## 8. Question 8 — Integration model onto the IRIS wire + DEC-EMERG-XXXX recommendations

### 8.1 Wire facts (code-read, Level 4 internal)

- `ContentType::Sos = 2`, `ContentType::EmergencyAlert = 14` exist in `protocol/content_type.rs`; `EMERGENCY_BROADCAST` recipient constant (4-byte) exists in `protocol/mod.rs`.
- Flat CBOR envelope: integer keys 1-18, Core Deterministic Encoding (RFC 8949 §4.2); signature covers fields 1-14 except hop_count(8)+signature(15); fields 16+ unsigned; unknown keys >18 ignored (append-only evolution).
- `P0_MAX_ENVELOPE_BYTES = 255`; **P0 payload ceiling = 84 B** (DISC-0008: 237-byte measured envelope, 64-byte sig dominates).
- `fragment.rs`: P0-P3 + EmergencyAlert are **excluded from fragmentation**; MAX_FRAGMENTS = 2.
- `ack.rs` retry: P0 30 s / 2× / unlimited-until-TTL; P1 60 s; P2 120 s; P3 300 s; cap 1 h.
- `message_engine`: P0 SOS broadcast / empty-recipient envelopes **never encrypted** (plaintext by design).
- IDENT-001 provides: self-authenticating PeerId (Ed25519), RED-0008 SPKI key-anchored `verify_chain` (cap 8, no PKIX), TrustStore (TOFU + verified), rotation/null-rotation revocation.

### 8.2 Conflicts / corrections surfaced by research (must be resolved in DESIGN)

- **C1 — Broadcast size vs envelope**: EMERGENCY_BROADCAST.md allows `instructions ≤2000 chars` — cannot fit 255-B envelope, and EmergencyAlert is excluded from fragmentation. v1 must either cap alert content to envelope-sized (headline only + compact instructions) with a follow-up P3 detail message, or carve an exception for authority broadcasts (rejected: breaks fragment.rs invariant + E2EE seam). **Recommend v1: compact alert in envelope; details as signed follow-up P3.**
- **C2 — EMERG-001 acceptance criteria do not exist yet**: `PROJECT_GRAPH.yaml` EMERG-001 node has no `acceptance_criteria` block (ACCEPTANCE_POLICY `applies_to` includes EMERG-001; universal criteria apply). **DESIGN must define ACs before IMPLEMENT.**
- **C3 — Tone correction**: EMERGENCY_UX.md 880/660 Hz pattern is not a standard WEA/international alert tone; standard WEA signal is 853+960 Hz (§6). Correct at DESIGN.
- **C4 — Legal update**: EMERGENCY_ABUSE.md IPC refs → BNS (§7; IPC §153A mapping unverified).
- **C5 — SOS cadence doc claim**: "15-min retransmit" (if present) ≠ implemented ack.rs P0 retry (30 s/2×/unlimited-until-TTL); align docs to implementation.
- **C6 — "X.509-inspired" language** (EMERGENCY_GOVERNANCE AuthorityCertificate) vs IDENT-001 D6/RES-0016 "no PKIX": the *structure* (issuer/subject/validity/scope/permissions/signature, CBOR-serialized) is fine and desirable; the *constraint* is: no X.509/PKIX parsing crates (rustls-webpki family), key-anchored SPKI semantics (RFC 9804), RED-0008 reuse. Reword governance doc header to "SPKI-style key-anchored certificate, CBOR-serialized".

### 8.3 RUSTSEC / D6 — no new crypto or runtime crates needed for EMERG-001 (2026-08-15 snapshot)

- All emergency logic uses existing stack: `ed25519-dalek 2.x` (verify_strict only), `sha2`, CBOR codec, ISTORE queues, SCF, ROUTE-001/002. **No new dependencies required**; D6 (no new crypto crates) holds.
- RUSTSEC posture unchanged from RES-0015/RES-0016 snapshot: no new advisory vs pinned set (x25519-dalek 2.0.1, ed25519-dalek 2.x, chacha20poly1305 0.10.1, hkdf 0.12, curve25519-dalek ≥4.1.3). No PKIX family (rustls-webpki RUSTSEC-2026-0104/0098/0099, pemfile RUSTSEC-2025-0134) is introduced by the authority-cert design (SPKI key chains need only Ed25519 verify). Geo/area-code handling uses plain strings (ISO 3166-2) — no geo crate needed. TTS/accessibility output deferred to platform nodes (ANDROID-001/IOS-001).

### 8.4 Recommended decision inputs (DEC-EMERG-XXXX candidates for DESIGN, to be formalized as DEC records)

- **DEC-EMERG-0001 — Alert wire format**: minimal **CAP-inspired CBOR subset** on the existing envelope (fields: broadcast_id[16], authority_id, cert_chain (SPKI), area_code (ISO 3166-2), severity, message_type, language (BCP 47), headline (compact), instructions (compact), issued_at, expires_at). CAP/ITU-T X.1303bis is semantic reference only; no CAP-XML/PER on wire.
- **DEC-EMERG-0002 — Authority identity**: reuse IDENT-001 RED-0008 SPKI key-anchored chain (cap 8; hierarchy realistic max 4 per governance); validity 2y/1y/6mo/3mo per EMERGENCY_GOVERNANCE (no external benchmark — justify via offline-revocation tradeoff + governance doc, Gap G5); preloaded NDMA root; offline verify everywhere; no PKIX crates.
- **DEC-EMERG-0003 — SOS semantics**: P0 plaintext broadcast (ContentType::Sos, empty-recipient path, payload ≤84 B); location + timestamp + compact message + (optional) contact hint; encrypted direct SOS to known peers stays on the normal message path.
- **DEC-EMERG-0004 — Cancel/status**: signed **BroadcastCancel** propagated like the broadcast, within expires window; **status reporting OFF by default** (RFC 9171 §6.2); SOS ack = application message; typed admin-record style for cancel (RFC 9713 model); no per-relay ACK for broadcasts (anti-probing).
